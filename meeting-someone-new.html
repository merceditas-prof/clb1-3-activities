Introductions -- "in" or "on", and I / my / he / his / she / her

"In" or "On"?

IN

Use in when there is no exact day:

  • a year -- in 2019
  • a month -- in May
  • a month + year -- in May 2019

ON

Use on with an exact date:

  • day + month -- on May 10
  • day + month + year -- on May 10, 2019
I came to Canada in May 2019. (month + year, no exact day)
I came to Canada on May 10, 2019. (exact date)

Two Kinds of Words

In the conversation you say I and you say my. They are not the same. Look at the difference.

I, YOU, HE, SHE

This is the person who does something.

An action word comes after it:

  • I am from Peru.
  • You are from Iran.
  • He is from Syria.
  • She came to Canada in 2021.

MY, YOUR, HIS, HER

This shows the thing belongs to the person.

A thing comes after it:

  • My name is Ana.
  • Your name is Sara.
  • His wife is from Syria.
  • Her family lives in Mexico.
Easy way to remember:
After my / your / his / her there is always a THING (name, family, husband, country).
After I / you / he / she there is always an ACTION WORD (am, are, is, come, live).

Watch Out! Common Mistakes

NO: He name is Ali.    YES: His name is Ali.

"Name" is a thing, so we need his.

NO: My am from Peru.    YES: I am from Peru.

"Am" is an action word, so we need I.

NO: Her came to Canada in 2018.    YES: She came to Canada in 2018.

"Came" is an action word, so we need she.

NO: What is you name?    YES: What is your name?

"Name" is a thing, so we need your.

Read the Conversation

Two students meet for the first time. Read it with a partner. Then change places and read it again.

A Hi. My name is        . What is your name?

B My name is        .

A Where are you from?

B I am from        . And you, where are you from?

A I am from        .

B When did you come to Canada?

A I came to Canada        . And you?

B I came to Canada        .

A Nice to meet you!

B Nice to meet you, too!

Blue words = the person (I, you).
Orange words = the thing belongs to the person (my, your).
Remember the date: I came to Canada in 2019. I came to Canada on May 10, 2021.

Part 1: Speaking Practice -- Breakout Rooms

Go to your breakout room. Take turns with your partner. Use the sentences below and fill in your own information.

My name is         .

I am from         .

I came to Canada in / on         .

You can also say:

  • I came to Canada with my family in / on ____.
  • I came to Canada with my brother in / on ____.
  • I came to Canada with my sister in / on ____.
  • I came to Canada with my husband in / on ____.
  • I came to Canada with my wife in / on ____.
  • I came to Canada with my child in / on ____.
  • I came to Canada with my children in / on ____.

If you don't hear your partner, ask: "Pardon?"

Then say your sentence again.

When you meet someone new, say: "Nice to meet you."

Your partner answers: "Nice to meet you too."

Remember: in = month or year. on = exact date.

Can Do Checklist

After your breakout room practice, check Yes or No for each sentence.

1. I can answer introduction questions from a classmate.

2. I answer in complete sentences. I say, "My name is ___. I am from ___. I came to Canada in / on ___."

3. I use "in" for months and years, for example, "I came to Canada in May 2019." I use "on" for dates, for example, "I came to Canada on May 10, 2019."

4. I say my sentence again when my classmate asks, "Pardon?"

5. My new classmate says, "Nice to meet you." I say, "Nice to meet you too."

6. I use I, you, he and she before an action word. I say, "He is from Syria."

7. I use my, your, his and her before a thing. I say, "His name is Ali."

8. I can tell the class about my classmate. I say, "His name is ___. He is from ___. He came to Canada in ___."
