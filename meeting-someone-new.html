When did you come to Canada? -- "in" or "on"

"In" or "On"?

IN

Use in when there is no exact day:

  • a year -- in 2019
  • a month -- in May
  • a month + year -- in May 2019

ON

Use on with an exact date:

  • day + month -- on May 10
  • day + month + year -- on May 10, 2019
I came to Canada in May 2019. (month + year, no exact day)
I came to Canada on May 10, 2019. (exact date)

Speaking Practice -- Breakout Rooms

Go to your breakout room. Take turns with your partner. Use the sentences below and fill in your own information.

My name is         .

I am from         .

I came to Canada in / on         .

You can also say:

  • I came to Canada with my family in / on ____.
  • I came to Canada with my brother in / on ____.
  • I came to Canada with my sister in / on ____.
  • I came to Canada with my husband in / on ____.
  • I came to Canada with my wife in / on ____.
  • I came to Canada with my child in / on ____.
  • I came to Canada with my children in / on ____.

If you don't hear your partner, ask: "Pardon?"

Then say your sentence again.

When you meet someone new, say: "Nice to meet you."

Your partner answers: "Nice to meet you too."

Remember: in = month or year. on = exact date.

Can Do Checklist

After your breakout room practice, check Yes or No for each sentence.

1. I can answer introduction questions from a classmate.

2. I answer in complete sentences. I say, "My name is ___. I am from ___. I came to Canada in / on ___."

3. I use "in" for months and years, for example, "I came to Canada in May 2019." I use "on" for dates, for example, "I came to Canada on May 10, 2019."

4. I say my sentence again when my classmate asks, "Pardon?"

5. My new classmate says, "Nice to meet you." I say, "Nice to meet you too."
